# 04 Style guide: writing for Uplift modules

Learners are busy teachers and staff, often taking training between other tasks. Every word on screen should help them understand something or do something.

## Voice

- **Warm, direct and practical.** Write like a helpful colleague, not a policy manual.
- **Talk to the learner as "you".** Use "we" for Uplift ("We ask that you…").
- **Respect their expertise.** Learners are professionals. Explain the new idea, not their own job.
- **Be clear about rules.** When something is required, say so plainly: "Never put scholar data into a personal AI account."

## Plain language

- Aim for a **grade 8 reading level** or lower.
- Keep sentences **short**, ideally under 20 words, with one idea per sentence.
- Use **common words**: "use" not "utilize", "help" not "facilitate", "about" not "approximately".
- **Explain jargon** the first time, or drop it. "Hallucination (when AI states something false as if it's true)."
- Put the **most important point first**, in the heading if you can.
- Keep **on-screen text short**. Put detail in fold boxes.

## Headings and labels

- **Sentence case**: "Privacy comes first", not "Privacy Comes First". Proper nouns keep their capitals.
- Headings **say the point**, not just the topic: "Your account type decides what's protected" is better than "Account types".
- Goals and objectives **start with a verb**: Explain, Choose, Protect, Write.

### Standard interface words

Use these words exactly, so every module feels the same:

| Use | Not |
|---|---|
| **Next**, **Previous** | Continue, Back, Forward |
| **Menu**, **Resources**, **Settings** | Navigation, Links, Options |
| **Check my answer** | Submit, Done |
| **Try again** | Retry, Restart |
| **Play animation**, **Pause**, **Replay** | Start, Go |
| **Close this window** | Exit, Quit |
| **Select** | Click, Tap (they assume one kind of device) |

## Uplift terms

| Use | Notes |
|---|---|
| **scholars** | Uplift's word for students in learner-facing text. "Student data" is fine in legal terms like FERPA. |
| **staff**, **teachers**, **campus staff**, **central office** | The three role groups used in role pickers |
| **Uplift Education** | The organization, and the FERPA-compliant district tools |
| Tool names exactly as in policy | For example **Microsoft Copilot** (with your Uplift account). Check the module's approved-tools list against current policy. |
| **campus leader**, **Academic Directors**, **Dean** | Use current Uplift role titles. Check them with the content owner. |

## Examples and fictional data

- **Every name, scenario, email and number in an example must be fictional.** Never use real scholars, staff, families, campuses' private data or real records.
- Choose names from a **range of backgrounds**, and avoid names of well-known people.
- Keep fictional examples **realistic**: real tasks staff do (a family newsletter, a survey summary, an event reminder).
- Mark fictional data as fictional where it might be mistaken for real (for example "Fictional example").
- Statistics are **real and sourced**: say who, when and how many ("October 2026 staff survey, 160 respondents").

## Prompts in examples

When a module teaches prompting, write prompts in **CRAFT order: Context, Role, Action, Format, Tone**. Fold any extra parts (required items, length, audience) into **Action**.

## Feedback and instructions

- **Instructions** say what to do and what happens next: "Sort each example into a box. Next unlocks when all four are sorted."
- **Right-answer feedback** confirms and reinforces: "Right. Only Copilot with your Uplift account is approved for scholar data."
- **Wrong-answer feedback** is kind and specific. Show the correct answer, and don't blame the learner.
- **Never rely on color, position or sound alone**: don't write "the green button" or "the box on the right".

## Numbers, dates and links

- Use numerals: "3 questions", "25 minutes", "95%".
- Dates: "October 2026" or "November 9, 2026".
- Link text says where it goes: "Uplift AI Policy on Uplink", never "click here".
- Email addresses are written out in full: `ai@uplifteducation.org`.

## Punctuation

- No semicolons in learner-facing text. Make two sentences instead.
- Use the serial comma only when it prevents confusion.
- Use straight, simple punctuation. Avoid exclamation marks except for a rare celebration ("Mission complete!").
